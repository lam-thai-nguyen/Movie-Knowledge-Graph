\section{Data collection and transformation}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Data collection}

The movie dataset is collected from TMDB, a public
source of movie metadata. The collection focuses on the first five pages of
TMDB's popular-movie listing, which return 100 movies in total. 
For each listed movie, detailed metadata and
credit information are retrieved. This produces a bounded snapshot of the
popular-movie catalogue rather than a complete crawl of TMDB.

The collected movie attributes include the TMDB and IMDb identifiers, title,
synopsis, original language, release date, runtime, average vote, and vote
count. The collection also records related entities: genres, production
companies, production countries, spoken languages, cast members, and
directors. Cast records retain the first ten credited actors, their character
names, and their order in the cast. Crew records retain directors and their
person identifiers. This selection prioritizes the information needed for
movie retrieval, recommendation-style queries, and relationship queries over
the entire credit list.

The result is organized as entity tables and relationship tables, under the CSV format. Entity
tables store one row per movie, genre, company, country, language, or person
reference, while relationship tables preserve many-to-many associations such
as movie--genre and movie--production-company links. This separation avoids
repeating descriptive values and provides a convenient intermediate
representation for RDF generation. Text values are trimmed, missing values
are represented as empty fields, and all files are written using UTF-8
encoding.

Table~\ref{tab:tmdb-data-structure} summarizes this organization. The first
row contains the central movie records, the second covers reusable
descriptive entities, and the third captures the available credit
information. The final row distinguishes relationship records from entity
records; these links are later converted into RDF predicates between stable
resources.

This organization also makes the limitations of the snapshot explicit.
Movies can occur more than once in the popular-movie results, and the
available metadata depends on the completeness of the source records. In
addition, restricting the cast to ten entries reduces the size of the graph
but does not represent the full cast of every movie.

\subsection{Data transformation}

The tabular data are transformed into an RDF graph using Schema.org
vocabulary. Each identified entity receives a stable URI composed of a
project base URI, an entity type, and its source identifier. For example,
movie, person, genre, company, country, and language resources are separated
by their respective URI paths. This URI design preserves the identity of
entities across files and enables relationships to be represented as links
between resources rather than duplicated text values.

Movies are represented as instances of \texttt{schema:Movie}. Their
descriptive fields are mapped to properties such as
\texttt{schema:name}, \texttt{schema:abstract},
\texttt{schema:datePublished}, and \texttt{schema:duration}. Related
entities are represented as typed resources: genres as
\texttt{schema:Genre}, people as \texttt{schema:Person}, production
companies as \texttt{schema:Organization}, countries as
\texttt{schema:Country}, and languages as \texttt{schema:Language}.
Relationship tables consequently become graph edges such as
\texttt{schema:genre}, \texttt{schema:productionCompany},
\texttt{schema:countryOfOrigin}, and \texttt{schema:inLanguage}.

Values are assigned RDF datatypes where appropriate. Release dates use
\texttt{xsd:date}, runtimes are represented as \texttt{xsd:duration}, rating
values use \texttt{xsd:decimal}, and vote counts and cast positions use
\texttt{xsd:integer}. Empty source values are omitted instead of being
emitted as empty literals. Ratings are represented by an anonymous
\texttt{schema:AggregateRating} resource so that the rating value and vote
count remain grouped. Similarly, actor information is reified as
\texttt{schema:Role} resources when character names or cast positions are
available.

listing of the resource. The first group of statements describes the movie
itself and demonstrates how dates, durations, and ratings are typed. The
genre and director objects are URI links to other resources in the graph,
whereas the \texttt{schema:sameAs} objects link the local resource to its
TMDB and IMDb counterparts. The final group shows why a role resource is
useful: it preserves both the actor identity and the character name while
also retaining the cast position.
